# OKR Guide

How we write objectives and key results. The OKRs themselves live in [current-quarter.md](current-quarter.md) — read this when drafting or refreshing that file, typically once a quarter.

## Good Objectives

- Qualitative and inspirational
- Time-bound (quarterly)
- Something the team can actually influence

## Good Key Results

- Measurable — a number, with a baseline and a target
- Ambitious but achievable
- Outcome-focused, not task-focused

**Examples:**

❌ Bad: Ship dark mode (task)
✅ Good: Increase D7 retention from 40% to 55% (outcome)

❌ Bad: Improve user experience (vague)
✅ Good: Reduce time-to-first-value from 10 min to 3 min (measurable)

## Laddering

Every product-team KR names the company objective it serves. If a KR can't say which company OKR it moves and how, it's either mislabeled work or the wrong bet:

- KR 1 → Company OKR: [which one and how]
- KR 2 → Company OKR: [which one and how]

## Scoring and Confidence

- **70% rule:** aim for ~70% completion. Hitting 100% means the targets weren't ambitious enough.
- Give each objective an owner and a confidence score (X/10) when setting it; revisit confidence mid-quarter rather than waiting for the retro.
